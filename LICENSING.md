# Licensing

libarude is dual licensed. You may use it under **either** of the following —
but the second one has to be arranged with me first.

## Option 1 — GNU AGPLv3 (no need to ask)

The default. The full text is in [LICENSE](LICENSE); the SPDX identifier is
`AGPL-3.0-or-later`.

You may use, modify and distribute libarude at no cost, including commercially.
In return, the AGPL requires that anything you build on it stays free software:

- If you distribute a program that incorporates libarude, you must offer the
  complete corresponding source of that program under the AGPL.
- If you let users interact with such a program **over a network**, you must
  offer them its complete corresponding source too. This network clause is what
  distinguishes the AGPL from the ordinary GPL, and it applies even when you
  never ship a binary to anyone.

Using libarude privately, without distributing anything and without exposing it
over a network, carries no such obligation.

## Option 2 — MIT, by arrangement

If the copyleft obligations above are incompatible with your project — typically
because you ship proprietary software or run a closed network service — I can
license the identical code to you under the MIT license instead. The MIT license
imposes no copyleft: you keep your own source closed.

This is **not** offered to the public unconditionally. There is deliberately no
MIT license file in this repository, because no such grant exists until we agree
terms. What I ask in exchange is negotiable and depends on who you are and what
you are building. It might be:

- a payment,
- sharing improvements or fixes back,
- an honourable mention or credit,
- or nothing at all.

Small projects, hobbyists and non-profits should not assume the answer is
expensive. Just ask.

**Contact: adrian@mav.ch**

A grant under this option, once agreed, is issued in writing and covers the
version(s) of libarude named in it.

## Why this works

I am the copyright holder of libarude. A copyright holder is not bound by the
terms they offer to others, so I am free to license the same code to different
people under different terms. The AGPL text in `LICENSE` is a grant *from me to
you*; it does not restrict what else I may grant to anyone else.

This is also why contributions carry the relicensing terms described in the
README. If part of the code were owned by someone who had released it to me only
under the AGPL, I could no longer offer the whole library under MIT.
